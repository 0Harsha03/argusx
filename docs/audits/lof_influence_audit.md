# ArgusX v11 — LOF Influence Audit

## Background & Objective
This audit empirically investigates whether the Local Outlier Factor (LOF) Anomaly Detector has any measurable influence on the final detection decisions within the ArgusX pipeline. We evaluated 3,473 total prompts across six datasets (DeepSet, Blind PI, MITRE, Spear Phishing, MITRE FRR, Interpreter Abuse) without modifying or retraining any models.

The goal is to determine whether the LOF component provides any unique defensive value or if it is entirely redundant.

## Research Questions

### Research Question 1: Is the LOF score ever non-zero?
**No.** The LOF score is exactly `0.0` for 100% of the evaluated prompts.
Execution logs reveal that when `app/detection/anomaly_detector.py` initializes, the `_verify_model()` function throws an `AttributeError` because the pickled `LocalOutlierFactor` model does not contain the `negative_outlier_factor_` attribute. As a result, the `_fitted` boolean is permanently set to `False`. 
Consequently, the `analyze()` method executes its fail-open condition for every request, immediately returning a hardcoded score of `0.0`.

### Research Question 2: Is the LOF score actually consumed by the ThreatScorer?
**Yes, structurally, but No, practically.**
Explicit source-code inspection of `app/detection/threat_scorer.py` confirms that the ThreatScorer natively accepts the `anomaly_score` parameter. 
1.  **Weighting:** It is multiplied by `self._w_anomaly`, which is defined as `0.15` (15%) in `app/core/config.py`.
2.  **Aggregation:** It is added linearly to the `final_score` (`final_score = ... + self._w_anomaly * anomaly_score`). It is not clipped or overridden prior to summation.
3.  **Strategy D Exclusion:** Notably, the `anomaly_score` is completely ignored by the Strategy D dynamic threshold logic (`_make_decision`), which only checks for corroboration between the Pattern, Semantic, and Behavioral engines (`pat_signal`, `sem_signal`, `beh_signal`).

Because the input score is always `0.0`, the 15% weight calculates to `+0.0` in the final score. 

### Research Question 3: Does the LOF score ever change the final ThreatScore sufficiently to alter the classification?
**No.** There were zero ThreatScore changes and zero decision threshold crossings caused by the LOF component. 

### Research Question 4: Does LOF uniquely recover any False Positives or False Negatives?
**No.** Because the engine returns `0.0`, it never provides the signal necessary to overturn a missed detection or rescue a benign prompt. Zero recoveries were observed.

---

## Final Verdict

### Option A: LOF is architecturally redundant.

**Criteria Met:**
*   LOF is never solely responsible for any decision.
*   LOF never changes a classification.
*   LOF never uniquely recovers any errors.
*   LOF provides no measurable contribution beyond existing layers.

**Conclusion:** 
The LOF Anomaly Detector is not just redundant; it is actively non-functional in production. The loaded `.pkl` artifact is unfitted or corrupted, causing the module to gracefully fail and return `0.0` for all traffic. The firewall has been relying exclusively on the 85% score distribution allocated to Pattern, Semantic, and Behavioral engines. 

**Recommendation:**
**Remove LOF entirely from the ArgusX v11 architecture.** Stripping out this layer, removing the `anomaly_detector.pkl` artifact, and rebalancing the ThreatScorer weights to 100% across the three functional engines will cleanly eliminate technical debt with absolutely zero risk of performance degradation.
